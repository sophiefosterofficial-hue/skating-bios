Glenn, Malinin, Efimova/Mitrofanov Headline 2026 U.S. Championships in St. Louis

January 11, 2026

ST. LOUIS — The 2026 Prevagen U.S. Figure Skating Championships delivered four days of compelling competition at the Enterprise Center, with Amber Glenn capturing her third consecutive senior ladies title, Ilia Malinin extending his dominance in men's singles, Alisa Efimova and Misha Mitrofanov repeating as pairs champions, and Madison Chock and Evan Bates claiming a seventh U.S. ice dance title together.

Ladies: Glenn Makes History, Tennell Returns to Form

Glenn won the ladies title with a combined score of 233.55, becoming the first woman since Michelle Kwan to win three consecutive U.S. ladies titles. Alysa Liu placed second with 229.14, and Isabeau Levito rounded out the podium at 224.45 in a tightly contested event that will determine three spots at the Olympic Winter Games.

Perhaps the most compelling story of the ladies competition came from Bradie Tennell, who claimed fourth place in her first U.S. Championship appearance in two seasons. The two-time national champion and 2018 Olympian delivered clean programs across both segments, finishing with 211.48 and demonstrating that her comeback is firmly on track entering the Olympic Games.

Sarah Everhardt, the reigning U.S. bronze medalist, placed fifth with 209.47 after a strong short program in which she ranked fourth. Everhardt, who earned her first national medal just a year ago, continues to establish herself among the country's elite.

Men: Malinin Unstoppable, Brown Earns Standing Ovation

Malinin won the men's title for the fourth consecutive year, posting a season-best 324.88 to extend his unbeaten streak in national competition. Andrew Torgashev placed second with 267.62, followed by Maxim Naumov in third at 249.16.

Jason Brown's performance in the short program drew one of the loudest ovations of the week, with his crowd-pleasing "Reel Around the Sun" routine earning 88.49. Brown, a two-time Olympian, finished eighth overall but continues to be one of the most celebrated performers in U.S. figure skating.

Pairs: Efimova and Mitrofanov Repeat, Future Looks Bright

Efimova and Mitrofanov won the pairs title for the second straight year with a total of 207.71, continuing to build toward what many expect will be a long era of U.S. pairs dominance. The team, which won Four Continents gold earlier this month, missed the Milan Olympics due to Mitrofanov's citizenship timeline but remains one of the top pairs teams in the world.

Ice Dance: Chock and Bates Cap Remarkable Career

Chock and Bates won their seventh U.S. ice dance title together, the final nationals of their extraordinary career before the Olympic Games. The couple, who won Olympic silver in Milan and three World titles, were visibly emotional during their free dance to close out a national title run unlike any in recent memory.
