U.S. Takes Home Four Medals at 2025 Finlandia Trophy; Juarez-Rivas Earns Second Career Senior Grand Prix Medal

Glenn finishes second in women's singles; Juarez-Rivas earns bronze in Helsinki for her second career senior Grand Prix podium
11/22/2025By: U.S. Figure Skating
Add photo
Getty Images

The United States took home four medals at the 2025 Finlandia Trophy, the sixth and final regular event of the 2025-26 ISU Grand Prix of Figure Skating, held November 21-22 at the Helsinki Ice Hall in Helsinki, Finland. Amber Glenn finished second in the women's event, and first-year senior Gabriela Juarez-Rivas claimed the bronze medal — the first senior Grand Prix podium finish of her career.

Japan's Mone Chiba won the women's title with 217.22 points, leaping from second after the short program with a top-scoring free skate of 144.33. Glenn, who had led after a personal-best short program of 75.72 despite battling a tooth issue and sinus infection throughout the week, finished second with 213.41 points.

"I haven't been feeling my best, I've been sleeping a lot since I got here and if anything, I felt slow and sluggish. Figure skating is a fast-paced sport, we're in the air for less than a second, it's so quick — I really had to find that in-between." — Amber Glenn

The headline result for Team USA, however, was Juarez-Rivas' bronze medal. The 17-year-old from Mountain View, California, competing in only her second Grand Prix assignment of the season, delivered a confident free skate to finish third overall with 203.15 points — her second senior Grand Prix podium finish, following a bronze at the 2024 Grand Prix de France — and punching her ticket to the Vancouver Grand Prix Final.

"To be on the podium at a Grand Prix — that's something I've been working toward since I started seniors. I just wanted to go out and skate my program. It happened to be enough today." — Gabriela Juarez-Rivas

The result also confirmed Juarez-Rivas' qualification for the ISU Grand Prix of Figure Skating Final in Vancouver, where she would go on to finish fourth — a remarkable result in her debut Grand Prix Final appearance.

In pairs, Ellie Kam and Danny O'Shea earned bronze, finishing just behind Germany's Hase and Volodin (gold) and Efimova and Mitrofanov (silver) in a pairs event that saw less than one point separate the top four teams going into the free skate. In ice dance, Laurence Fournier Beaudry and Guillaume Cizeron of France won gold, with Madison Chock and Evan Bates earning silver.
