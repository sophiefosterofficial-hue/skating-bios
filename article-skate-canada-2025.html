Tennell, Everhardt, Levito Lead U.S. Women at 2025 Skate Canada International

November 2, 2025

SASKATOON — Three of the United States' most compelling women's stories of the Olympic season played out at the 2025 Skate Canada International, as Bradie Tennell, Sarah Everhardt, and Isabeau Levito each took to the ice at Sasktel Centre in performances that underscored just how deep the American ladies field has become.

Tennell, the two-time U.S. champion returning from an extended absence from the Grand Prix Series, delivered a short program to "Young and Beautiful" by Lana Del Rey that showed sharpened technical focus alongside the elegance that has defined her career. Her free skate to "The Mission" by Ennio Morricone was one of the highlights of the competition and she earned her first Grand Prix medal in several seasons.

"Being out here again on this stage feels incredible," Tennell said after her short program. "I missed competing internationally. I want to prove that I belong here."

Everhardt, the reigning U.S. bronze medalist who opened her season with a strong result at the Lombardia Trophy, continued to build on her breakthrough 2024–25 campaign. The Manassas, Virginia, native trains alongside other Washington FSC skaters in the Northern Virginia area and has quietly developed into one of the most well-rounded American ladies competitors.

Levito, the 2024 World silver medalist, arrived in Saskatoon seeking her first Grand Prix gold of the season after a fourth-place result at the Grand Prix de France the week prior. The reigning U.S. bronze medalist delivered a strong free skate to move into medal contention.

In the men's event, Lucas Broussard made his senior Grand Prix debut alongside Ilia Malinin, who extended his win streak to twelve consecutive individual competition victories. Broussard, the 2023 U.S. junior champion and 2024 CS Cranberry Cup gold medalist, earned strong marks in both segments and showed flashes of the technical firepower that have made him one of the most anticipated senior debutants in recent years.

Carreira and Ponomarenko, Bratti and Somerville, and Neset and Markelov all competed in the ice dance event, with Carreira and Ponomarenko using the early-season competition to debut their new rhythm dance to the 1990s theme that defines this season's required pattern.
