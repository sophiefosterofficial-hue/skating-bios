Team USA Makes History with Three World Titles at 2025 Championships in Boston

March 30, 2025

BOSTON — In the most dominant performance by a U.S. team at the World Figure Skating Championships since the sport's modern era began, Team USA claimed gold in three of four disciplines at the TD Garden on Saturday, capping a historic week on home ice that few in attendance will forget.

Alysa Liu won the women's title Friday night with a total score of 222.97, becoming the first American woman to claim a World Championship since Kimmie Meissner in 2006. Ilia Malinin defended his men's title by more than 30 points with a season-best 318.56. And Madison Chock and Evan Bates completed an unprecedented third consecutive ice dance World Championship, the first ice dance team worldwide to accomplish the feat since Oksana Grishuk and Evgeni Platov in the 1990s.

Liu: "I Don't Know How I Did This"

Liu's victory stood as perhaps the weekend's most stunning result. Sitting in the kiss and cry beside coaches Massimo Scali and Laura Lipetsky after her free skate, the 19-year-old watched her score appear on the screen — 148.39 for a 222.97 total — and appeared genuinely speechless. "What the hell?" she said with a disbelieving smile, the words picked up by the broadcast.

The win came less than a year after Liu returned to competition following a two-year retirement and represented her first international gold medal at the senior level. "I'm not going to lie, this is an insane story," she said moments later. "I don't know how I came back to be world champion."

Malinin Extends Unbeaten Streak

Malinin, who has not lost an individual competition since the 2023 Grand Prix Final, delivered another staggering free skate at TD Garden — six quadruple jumps, a signature raspberry twist combination, and a late-program one-footed backflip that drew the loudest roar of the night. His free skate score of 208.15 was a season's international best and the only score above 200 points in the men's competition.

It was his second consecutive World title and placed him among an exclusive group of repeat men's world champions that includes Nathan Chen, Scott Hamilton, and Dick Button. Jason Brown, skating a breathtaking free skate to "Spiegel im Spiegel" by Arvo Pärt, rallied from 12th in the short program to finish eighth and earned a standing ovation that may have been the emotional highlight of the week.

Chock and Bates Make History

Chock and Bates, who married in 2024 after skating together for 15 years, completed their third consecutive ice dance title with trademark precision and deep emotional connection. The performance marked the first time a U.S. ice dance team has won three World titles and the first triple by any team in nearly three decades.

"It means everything," Bates said afterward. "We've worked for this for so long and to do it here in front of a home crowd, in Boston — I can't really put it into words."

Juarez-Rivas Makes Her Mark

Among the other notable U.S. performances was Gabriela Juarez-Rivas, the 17-year-old Mountain View, California, native competing in her first senior World Championship. Coming off a breakthrough 2024–25 season that included Grand Prix wins at Cup of China and Grand Prix de France, plus a silver at the Grand Prix Final in Grenoble, Juarez-Rivas delivered a composed performance to finish sixth with a total score of 206.76.

The result, while not on the podium, signaled the arrival of a genuine title contender — one who, in the season ahead, would prove far more difficult to overlook. "She handled the moment really well," coach Olga Ganicheva said after the free skate. "For a first senior Worlds, this is exactly the kind of experience you want."

The United States' three-discipline sweep was the first in the history of the World Championships, a result that set the tone for what would become an Olympic season like no other for Team USA.
